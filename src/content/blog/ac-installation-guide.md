A new air conditioner is a significant investment, but the purchase is only one part of the process. Correct installation can influence comfort, drainage, airflow, noise and long-term operation. Whether you are installing an AC at home, in an office or in a commercial space, it is useful to understand the basic checks that should happen before the technician starts work.

![AC installation guide](/images/ac-installation-guide.svg)

## Choose the indoor-unit location carefully

The indoor unit should be positioned where cooled air can circulate through the room effectively. Avoid locations where furniture, curtains or structural elements block airflow. The mounting surface should also be suitable for securely supporting the unit.

The exact placement depends on the room layout and the AC manufacturer's installation requirements. A technician should consider practical access for future cleaning and maintenance as well.

## Plan the outdoor-unit location

The outdoor unit releases heat, so it needs adequate ventilation. It should be installed on a stable and suitable support that allows the unit to operate safely and gives technicians reasonable access for maintenance.

Avoid placing the condenser in a location where airflow is heavily restricted by walls, stored materials or other equipment. The final clearance requirements should follow the manufacturer's specifications.

## Check the drainage route

Condensation from the indoor unit needs a reliable drainage path. The drain pipe should be routed so water can flow correctly and should be installed without unnecessary bends or low points that can encourage blockage.

After installation, the technician should check that water drains correctly. This simple test can help reduce the chance of leakage later.

## Consider the refrigerant pipe route

The connection between the indoor and outdoor units should be planned neatly and according to the AC manufacturer's requirements. Excessive bends, poor insulation or an unsuitable route can affect the installation quality.

The refrigerant lines should be properly insulated and protected. Installation should be performed by a trained technician using the correct tools and procedures.

## Electrical safety matters

An AC can require a dedicated electrical arrangement depending on its capacity and the manufacturer's specifications. The power supply, protective devices and wiring should be suitable for the equipment.

Electrical work should not be improvised. If changes to the electrical supply are required, use a qualified professional and follow applicable safety standards.

## Test the system after installation

Before considering the job complete, the system should be operated and checked. Cooling performance, airflow, drainage, unusual noise and the outdoor unit's operation should all be observed.

The customer should also receive basic guidance on remote-control operation, temperature settings, filter cleaning and routine maintenance.

## Keep the installation neat

A professional installation should not only work correctly; it should also look organised. Pipe insulation, drainage and wiring should be routed neatly without creating unnecessary visual clutter. Good finishing makes future maintenance easier too.

Take a few minutes to inspect the final work from both inside and outside before the technician leaves.

## Installation is the foundation of AC performance

Even a high-quality AC depends on correct installation. Location, airflow, drainage, electrical safety and system testing all contribute to a better result.
