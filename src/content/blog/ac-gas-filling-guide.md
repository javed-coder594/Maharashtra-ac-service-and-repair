“Gas filling” is a common phrase used when an air conditioner is not cooling properly, but weak cooling does not automatically mean that refrigerant needs to be added. An AC is a sealed system, so a low refrigerant level can indicate a leak or another technical issue. Proper diagnosis should come before any refrigerant-related work.

![AC gas filling and cooling performance guide](/images/ac-gas-filling.svg)

## What does refrigerant do?

Refrigerant is part of the cooling cycle that allows the AC to move heat from inside the room to the outdoor environment. The refrigerant charge needs to be within the appropriate range for the particular system.

If the charge is too low, cooling performance may be affected. But the correct amount depends on the model and installation specifications, so technicians should use the manufacturer's information and suitable measuring procedures.

## Does every weak-cooling AC need gas filling?

No. Weak cooling can result from several causes. Dirty filters, restricted airflow, dirty coils, poor installation conditions, sensor problems or other component issues can all affect cooling.

That is why a technician should inspect the AC before recommending refrigerant work. Adding refrigerant without understanding why the level is low may not solve the underlying problem.

## A possible refrigerant issue

If an AC has gradually lost cooling performance and other basic causes have been ruled out, the refrigerant circuit may need inspection. A technician can assess the system using appropriate tools and procedures.

If a leak is found, the priority should be understanding and addressing the leak rather than treating repeated gas filling as a permanent solution.

## Why professional diagnosis matters

Refrigerant service involves specialised equipment and should be handled by trained technicians. The type of refrigerant, operating pressures and charging procedure can vary between systems.

Guessing the refrigerant quantity or using an incorrect product can cause performance and equipment problems. Professional service helps ensure that the work matches the AC manufacturer's requirements.

## What should you ask before booking?

Ask the technician to explain why refrigerant service is being recommended. You can also ask whether the AC has been inspected for airflow, filters, coils, drainage and other common causes of poor cooling.

If a leak is suspected, ask what inspection is planned and whether additional repair work may be required.

Clear communication makes it easier to understand the difference between routine service and a technical repair.

## How can you notice a developing cooling problem?

Watch for gradual changes rather than waiting for complete failure. If the AC runs for long periods but the room remains uncomfortable, airflow becomes weaker or cooling is noticeably different from normal, arrange an inspection.

Also pay attention to unusual sounds, ice formation or repeated performance problems. These symptoms can have multiple causes, so they should be assessed in context.

## Avoid treating gas filling as routine maintenance

Refrigerant is not the same as a routine cleaning task. An AC does not normally need refrigerant added simply because a certain amount of time has passed. If the system is properly sealed and operating correctly, the refrigerant charge should remain within the intended system conditions.

The right approach is diagnosis first, repair where required and then correct refrigerant service if it is genuinely needed.

## Make the service decision based on diagnosis

If your AC is not cooling, do not book a refrigerant top-up blindly. Book an inspection and ask for the reason behind the recommendation.
